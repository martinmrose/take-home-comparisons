Ltd company optimisations
What are these?

These are operating setup choices that affect how the company is structured and taxed — not standard assumptions for every Ltd company. Second salary: paying a second director or employee is always a company cost (it reduces the profit available as dividends). If they earn at least £5,000 the company qualifies for the £10,500 Employment Allowance, which offsets employer NI on both salaries. Use the toggle to set who they are: include a spouse/partner (their net pay is added to household take-home, using a second personal allowance), or exclude an arm's-length employee (the cost still applies, but their pay isn't your income). Expenses: costs the company meets on your behalf that you would otherwise pay personally from net pay — e.g. phone, home office, professional subscriptions.

Assumptions & methodology — read this before trusting a number

This is a planning tool, not tax advice. It models the headline mechanics for a single person with no other income, no pension contributions and no student loan. Rates are 2025/26 and do not include Scottish income tax bands.

What the x-axis means — and the multiplier (k)

  • Bottom axis = gross annual salary £. This is the master scale.
  • Top axis = day rate, but each day rate is positioned at its salary-equivalent, not a literal ×days conversion. The salary-equivalent = day rate × billable days × k, where k is a single structural multiplier.
  • Why k exists: a £500/day contract and a £110k salary are not the same offer — they're taxed very differently. Lining them up 1:1 (the old behaviour) understated how a day rate maps to a salary. Instead, k is chosen by least-squares so the Ltd Outside IR35 line overlaps the permanent-salary line: this slides the day-rate ruler so reading "£500/day ≈ £130k salary" reflects equivalent take-home, not equivalent gross.
  • Auto-fit recomputes k by least-squares whenever you change household view, billable days or expenses — drag the slider to override it manually. Because the household toggle is so influential, the fit lands around k ≈ ×1.06 with household on but ≈ ×0.91 with it off (where your personal Ltd net actually trails an equivalent salary). The live value is shown next to the slider.
  • The curves cross — so no single k overlaps them end-to-end. Through the mid-range the Ltd Outside route carries a premium, but above roughly £800–£900/day it reverses: at additional-rate level, £1 of profit taken as dividends nets ~45p (25% corporation tax then 39.35% dividend tax) versus ~53p for salary (45% + 2% NI). On top of that the 60% allowance taper (£100k–£125,140), the NI band at £50,270 and corp-tax marginal relief bend the lines. Least-squares therefore picks a mid-range compromise. If you want the £400–£600/day band to sit exactly on the salary line (the most common comparison point), nudge the slider to about ×1.18. It is a deliberately rough, single-number equivalence — read it as such.
  • Only Ltd Outside IR35 is fitted to overlap salary. Umbrella and Inside IR35 shift by the same k, so they sit below the salary line — correctly showing they're worth less than the equivalent permanent job.

Ltd company — Outside IR35

  • You take a director's salary of £12,570 (tax-free, no employee NI).
  • A second salary (default £12,570, editable) is paid to a second director or employee. This is always a company cost that reduces the profit available as dividends. If they earn at least the £5,000 secondary threshold, the company qualifies for the Employment Allowance (£10,500), which offsets the employer NI on both salaries. Set the second salary to £0 (or below £5,000) and the allowance no longer applies, so the employer NI on your own salary becomes a real cost.
  • Company-paid expenses (default £5,000, editable) you would otherwise pay personally are met by the company. These are added directly to your after-tax income (you neither pay tax on them nor fund them from net pay).
  • Remaining profit is taxed at corporation tax (19% up to £50k, 25% above £250k, marginal relief between) and the rest distributed to you as dividends (£500 allowance, then 8.75% / 33.75% / 39.35%).
  • "Include in household income" OFF (default): only your personal net is plotted (salary + net dividends + expenses) — appropriate when the second salary goes to an arm's-length employee. ON: adds the second person's net pay (the income-splitting case, e.g. a spouse). Above their personal allowance their pay is taxed, so the addition is their take-home, not the gross salary.

Ltd company — Inside IR35

  • Modelled as an off-payroll deemed employment payment: the fee-payer deducts employer NI (15% above £5,000) and the 0.5% apprenticeship levy from the contract value before paying your deemed gross salary, which is then taxed as employment income (income tax + employee NI).
  • No dividends, no £5k expenses and no Employment Allowance apply. The result is essentially umbrella economics without the umbrella margin — so it sits just above the umbrella line.

Umbrella (PAYE)

  • The assignment rate (contract value) first loses the umbrella's margin (£/week × weeks worked).
  • From the remainder, employer NI (15% above £5,000) and the 0.5% apprenticeship levy are removed to leave your gross PAYE salary: gross = (pot + £750) / 1.155.
  • That gross is taxed as employment income (income tax + employee NI). No Employment Allowance (umbrellas can't apply it per worker).

Permanent PAYE salary

  • Gross salary taxed with income tax + employee NI. Personal allowance tapers £1 for every £2 over £100,000.
